✍️ Task 2 essay skeleton

A reliable four-paragraph structure.

  • Intro: paraphrase the question + state your position
  • Body 1: first main idea + explanation + example
  • Body 2: second main idea + explanation + example
  • Conclusion: restate position, summarise reasons

In-depth guides

Week 1 — Diagnose & learn formats. Take one full Reading and Listening mock to find your baseline. Learn every question type.

Week 2 — Skills & strategy. Focus on your two weakest skills. Do daily timed sections and review every mistake.

Week 3 — Writing & Speaking. Write one Task 1 and one Task 2 essay every other day; record a Speaking answer daily and get AI feedback.

Week 4 — Full mocks under timing. Sit complete tests at the same time of day as your real exam. Rest well the day before.

Reading: ~20 minutes per passage. If a question takes too long, guess, mark it, and move on.
Listening: Read questions ahead during pauses; transfer answers carefully at the end.
Writing: 20 minutes on Task 1, 40 on Task 2. Spend the first 5 minutes planning.
Speaking: In Part 2, use the full minute to note ideas for all four bullet points.

• Subject–verb agreement: “The number of students is rising” (not are).
• Articles: use a/an for singular countable nouns; don’t drop the before specific things.
• Prepositions: “an increase in prices”, “interested in”, “depend on”.
• Run-ons: join clauses with a conjunction or a full stop, not just a comma.
• Word form: “economic growth” (adj) vs “grew economically” (adv).

Open: “I’d like to talk about…”
Cover the bullets: answer each point in order, adding a detail or feeling to each.
Extend: add a reason, an example, or a comparison to keep talking for two minutes.
Close: “…and that’s why this experience meant so much to me.”
Use varied tenses and a couple of natural idioms — but never sound memorised.

Trends up: rose, increased, climbed, surged, grew.
Trends down: fell, declined, dropped, decreased, plummeted.
No change: remained stable, plateaued, levelled off.
Degree: sharply, significantly, gradually, slightly.
Always include a clear overview sentence with no specific numbers, then support it with selected key figures.
