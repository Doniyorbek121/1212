Reading strategies

The Academic Reading test gives you 60 minutes for three passages and 40 questions — roughly 20 minutes each. Time management is everything.

  • Skim first, read later. Spend 2 minutes getting the gist of each paragraph before you look at the questions.
  • Questions follow the passage order for most question types — locate answers sequentially.
  • True / False / Not Given: "False" contradicts the text; "Not Given" simply isn't mentioned. Never use outside knowledge.
  • Underline keywords in the question, then scan for synonyms in the text — answers are rarely word-for-word.
  • Don't leave blanks. There's no penalty for wrong answers, so always guess.

Listening strategies

Four sections, 40 questions, about 30 minutes of audio — played only once. Reading ahead is your superpower.

  • Read the questions before the audio starts. Predict what kind of word you need — a number, a name, a place.
  • Watch the word limit. "No more than two words" means three words is automatically wrong.
  • Listen for signposts like "however", "but actually", "the main reason" — answers often follow.
  • Beware of distractors. Speakers correct themselves ("It's 15… sorry, 50"). The last figure is usually right.
  • Check spelling. Misspelled answers lose the mark, even if you heard it correctly.

Writing strategies

Two tasks in 60 minutes. Task 2 is worth twice as much — spend 40 minutes on it and 20 on Task 1.

  • Answer the exact question. "Discuss both views and give your opinion" needs both views and your opinion.
  • Plan for 5 minutes. A clear structure scores higher than more words.
  • Use paragraphing: intro, 2 body paragraphs, conclusion. Each body paragraph = one main idea.
  • Show range in vocabulary and grammar — but accuracy beats showing off.
  • Hit the word count: 150+ for Task 1, 250+ for Task 2. Under the limit costs marks.

The 4 marking criteria

  • Task Achievement — did you fully answer?
  • Coherence & Cohesion — logical flow & linking
  • Lexical Resource — range & accuracy of vocabulary
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy — sentence variety

Speaking strategies

An 11–14 minute face-to-face interview in three parts. Fluency and natural language matter more than perfect grammar.

  • Extend every answer. Never stop at "yes" — add a reason and an example.
  • Part 2: use your 1 minute to jot keywords, then cover all four bullet points.
  • Paraphrase and use idioms naturally — but don't force memorised phrases.
  • Don't panic over mistakes. Self-correcting naturally shows control of the language.
  • Speak at a natural pace — hesitation fillers like "well, let me think" are fine.
